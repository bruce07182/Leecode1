Math Foundations for Physics C

Start with the idea, not the notation. These short lessons explain the calculus used in electricity and magnetism. Open only what you need, then return to your physics stage.

From basic to advanced

1 · What is a derivative?

A derivative measures how quickly a quantity changes at one instant. It is the slope of a curve.

dy/dx

Physics connection: If electric potential V changes with position x, the electric field along x is Eₓ = −dV/dx.

Example and answer

For V(x) = 12 − 3x, the slope dV/dx = −3, so Eₓ = +3 V/m.

2 · What is an integral?

An integral adds many tiny contributions. For a graph, a definite integral can represent signed area under the curve.

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx

Symbols: ∫ means sum continuously; a and b are the starting and ending limits; f(x) is what is being added; dx means a tiny interval of x.

Example and answer

∫₀² x dx = [x²/2]₀² = 2. Geometrically, this is the area of a triangle with base 2 and height 2.

3 · What is a dot product?

The dot product measures how much of one vector points along another.

A⃗ · B⃗ = AB cos θ

θ is the angle between the vectors. At 90°, the dot product is zero. In electric flux, this selects the field component perpendicular to the surface.

Example and answer

If E = 5 N/C, A = 2 m², and θ = 60° from the surface normal, flux = EA cosθ = 5 N·m²/C.

4 · What is a surface integral?

Instead of adding tiny lengths dx, a surface integral adds contributions over many tiny patches of a two-dimensional surface.

∫ₛ E⃗ · dA⃗

dA⃗ is a tiny area patch with a direction perpendicular to the surface. For curved surfaces, its direction changes from patch to patch.

Example and answer

If a uniform field crosses a flat surface perpendicularly, every patch contributes E dA, so total flux is EA.

5 · What is a closed-surface integral (∮)?

The small circle on the integral sign means the surface has no boundary or opening: imagine the entire outside of a sphere or sealed box.

∮ₛ E⃗ · dA⃗

It sums the outward flux through every tiny patch of the entire closed surface. This is the left side of Gauss's law. The symbol ∮ is commonly used for a closed surface in introductory physics, though in more advanced math a double integral with a circle may be used to emphasize the two-dimensional surface.

Why incoming and outgoing fields matter

The outward area vector is used at each patch. An electric field pointing outward contributes positive flux; inward contributes negative flux. Both can exist on one closed surface.

6 · What is Gauss's law saying mathematically?

The net flux over a whole closed surface is determined by the net charge enclosed, not by how the surface is shaped.

∮ₛ E⃗ · dA⃗ = Qenc / ε₀

Qenc is total charge inside; ε₀ is vacuum permittivity. If the charge distribution has enough symmetry, E can be taken outside the integral and the calculation becomes simple.

Example: spherical Gaussian surface

For a point charge Q at the center of a sphere of radius r, E is radial and has the same magnitude at every point. Thus ∮ E⃗ · dA⃗ = E(4πr²) = Q/ε₀, so E = Q/(4πε₀r²).

7 · What is a gradient (∇)?

A gradient describes how a scalar quantity changes with position in multiple directions.

E⃗ = −∇V

For one-dimensional motion this reduces to Eₓ = −dV/dx. The minus sign tells us the electric field points toward decreasing potential.

Notation note: ordinary integrals ∫, surface integrals ∫ₛ, and closed-surface integrals ∮ₛ are related ideas, but they sum over different geometric objects.
