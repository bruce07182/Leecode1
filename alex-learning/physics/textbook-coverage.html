Chapter 22 · Electric Fields

22-1 Electric field

E=F/q₀. Field lines leave positive charges, enter negative charges, and their density indicates field strength.

Optional advanced worked example

A negative test charge in a rightward field experiences a leftward force: F=qE.

22-2 Point charge

E=k|q|/r². Add fields as vectors; resolve x/y components.

Optional advanced worked example

Equal positive charges at x=±a give zero field at midpoint: equal opposite vectors.

22-3 Dipole field

Dipole moment p=qd points from − to +. Far axial field E≈2kp/r³.

Optional advanced worked example

At twice the far-axis distance, field is one-eighth as strong.

22-4 Line of charge

Split into dq=λdx and integrate dE=k dq/r² with directions.

Optional advanced worked example

Positive rod from x=a to b (a>0) gives Eₓ=−kλ(1/a−1/b) at origin.

22-5 Charged disk

Sum concentric rings. Axial E=(σ/2ε₀)(1−z/√(z²+R²)) for z>0.

Optional advanced worked example

Near a very large disk, z≪R, field approaches σ/(2ε₀).

22-6 Charge in electric field

F=qE; a=qE/m. Negative charge accelerates against the field.

Optional advanced worked example

Electron released in rightward E: vₓ=−eEt/m.

22-7 Dipole in field

Torque τ=pE sinθ; potential energy U=−pE cosθ.

Optional advanced worked example

At θ=90°, torque is maximum pE and energy is zero.

Chapter 23 · Gauss’s Law

23-1 Flux

Φ=∫E·dA. dA is normal to surface. Uniform flat: Φ=EA cosθ.

Optional worked example

E=200 N/C, A=0.30 m², θ=60°: Φ=30 N·m²/C.

23-2 Gauss law

∮E·dA=Qinside/ε₀. External charges have zero net flux.

Optional worked example

Enclosed +3 nC and −1 nC: Φ=2 nC/ε₀≈226 N·m²/C.

23-3 Conductor

At equilibrium E=0 inside metal, conductor is equipotential.

Optional worked example

Charged conductor can have nonzero potential despite E=0 within metal.

23-4 Cylindrical symmetry

A coaxial Gaussian cylinder gives the radial field of a long charged line: E = |lambda|/(2 pi epsilon0 r). End-cap flux vanishes.

Optional advanced problem

For lambda = 4 nC/m and r = 0.20 m, E is approximately 360 N/C, radially outward.

23-5 Planar symmetry

An infinite insulating sheet has field magnitude |sigma|/(2 epsilon0). Opposite parallel sheets reinforce between them and cancel outside.

Optional advanced problem

For equal +sigma and -sigma sheets, the field between is sigma/epsilon0 directed from positive to negative.

23-6 Spherical symmetry

Outside a spherical charge distribution E=kQ/r squared. Inside a uniform solid insulating sphere E=kQr/R cubed.

Optional advanced problem

Compare r=R/2 and r=2R: E values are kQ/(2R squared) and kQ/(4R squared), respectively.

Chapter 24 · Electric Potential

24-1 Potential, voltage and work

Potential V=U/q. Delta V is minus the path integral of E along displacement. Delta U=q Delta V; work by the field equals minus Delta U.

Optional advanced problem

A +2 microcoulomb charge moves through -30 volts: Delta U=-60 microjoules and the electric field does +60 microjoules of work.

24-2 Equipotential surfaces and field

Moving along an equipotential has Delta V=0. The electric field is perpendicular to an equipotential surface.

Optional advanced problem

Moving a charge along a circular equipotential around a point charge requires zero electrostatic work, even though a radial electric force exists.

24-3 Potential of a charged particle

For a point charge V=kq/r relative to infinity. Potential is scalar: add signed contributions without vector components.

Optional advanced problem

At a point equidistant from +Q and -Q, V=0. Electric field need not vanish because its contributions are vectors.

24-4 Dipole potential

Far from a dipole, V is approximately kp cos(theta)/r squared. The perpendicular bisector is at zero potential.

Optional advanced problem

At theta=90 degrees, V=0, but the dipole electric field is generally nonzero.
