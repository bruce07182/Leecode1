Chapter 22 · Electric Fields

22-1 Electric field

E=F/q₀. Field lines leave positive charges, enter negative charges, and their density indicates field strength.

Optional advanced worked example

A negative test charge in a rightward field experiences a leftward force: F=qE.

22-2 Point charge

E=k|q|/r². Add fields as vectors; resolve x/y components.

Optional advanced worked example

Equal positive charges at x=±a give zero field at midpoint: equal opposite vectors.

22-3 Dipole field

Dipole moment p=qd points from − to +. Far axial field E≈2kp/r³.

Optional advanced worked example

At twice the far-axis distance, field is one-eighth as strong.

22-4 Line of charge

Split into dq=λdx and integrate dE=k dq/r² with directions.

Optional advanced worked example

Positive rod from x=a to b (a>0) gives Eₓ=−kλ(1/a−1/b) at origin.

22-5 Charged disk

Sum concentric rings. Axial E=(σ/2ε₀)(1−z/√(z²+R²)) for z>0.

Optional advanced worked example

Near a very large disk, z≪R, field approaches σ/(2ε₀).

22-6 Charge in electric field

F=qE; a=qE/m. Negative charge accelerates against the field.

Optional advanced worked example

Electron released in rightward E: vₓ=−eEt/m.

22-7 Dipole in field

Torque τ=pE sinθ; potential energy U=−pE cosθ.

Optional advanced worked example

At θ=90°, torque is maximum pE and energy is zero.

Chapter 23 · Gauss’s Law

23-1 Flux

Φ=∫E·dA. dA is normal to surface. Uniform flat: Φ=EA cosθ.

Optional worked example

E=200 N/C, A=0.30 m², θ=60°: Φ=30 N·m²/C.

23-2 Gauss law

∮E·dA=Qinside/ε₀. External charges have zero net flux.

Optional worked example

Enclosed +3 nC and −1 nC: Φ=2 nC/ε₀≈226 N·m²/C.

23-3 Conductor

At equilibrium E=0 inside metal, conductor is equipotential.

Optional worked example

Charged conductor can have nonzero potential despite E=0 within metal.
