Unit 1

Essentials · 30-second summary

  • Charges exert forces: like repel, unlike attract.
  • Electric field tells the force on a positive test charge.
  • Gauss’s law relates total flux through a closed surface to enclosed charge.

Coulomb’s law · Ch. 21

Remember: Force grows with charge and falls as 1/r².

Why / when to use this

Two charges separated by twice the distance exert one-quarter the force.

Practice question

Try: if the original force is 12 N, what is it at double the distance?

Show answer and explanation

3 N, because 12 ÷ 4 = 3 N.

Electric field · Ch. 22

Remember: E = F/q; for a point charge, E = k|Q|/r².

Why / when to use this

Field direction is defined by the force on a positive test charge; a negative test charge feels force opposite the field.

Practice question

Try: a 2 μC positive test charge in a 300 N/C field feels what force?

Show answer and explanation

F = qE = 0.000002 × 300 = 0.0006 N, along the field.

Superposition and distributions · Ch. 22

Remember: Add electric fields as vectors, not just magnitudes.

Why / when to use this

For a line, disk, or other continuous charge distribution, break it into tiny charges dq and integrate their field contributions.

Practice question

Try: two identical positive charges lie symmetrically left and right of the midpoint. What is the net field there?

Show answer and explanation

Zero: equal and opposite field vectors cancel.

Electric flux · Ch. 23

Remember: Flux measures how much field passes through a surface.

Why / when to use this

For a flat area in uniform field, Φ = EA cos θ, where θ is between the field and the outward area normal.

Practice question

Try: the field is parallel to a flat surface. What is its flux?

Show answer and explanation

Zero, because the field is perpendicular to the area normal.

Gauss’s law · Ch. 23

Remember: Total outward electric flux through a closed surface equals Q enclosed / ε₀.

Why / when to use this

The law always holds, but it is easiest for calculating E with spherical, cylindrical, or planar symmetry. Charges outside can change local field but not net closed-surface flux.

Practice question

Try: double the enclosed charge. What happens to net flux?

Show answer and explanation

It doubles, regardless of the surface shape.

Tap any symbol to see its definition and units.

EElectric field strength

Force per unit positive test charge, measured in newtons per coulomb (N/C).

kCoulomb constant

Approximately 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².

QSource charge

The charge producing the electric field, measured in coulombs (C).

rDistance

Distance from the source charge to the observation point, in meters (m).

FElectric force

Force on the test charge, measured in newtons (N).

qTest charge

The charge experiencing the force, measured in coulombs (C).

∮Closed surface integral

Adds the electric flux over an entire closed surface.

SGaussian surface

An imaginary closed surface used to apply Gauss’s law.

dAArea vector element

A tiny area of the surface, pointing outward perpendicular to it.

QencEnclosed charge

Net electric charge inside the Gaussian surface.

ε₀Vacuum permittivity

Approximately 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²).

·Dot product

Only the component of electric field perpendicular to the surface contributes to flux.
