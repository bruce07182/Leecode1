Unit 1 — Electric Charges, Fields, and Gauss's Law

Idea: Like charges repel, opposite charges attract. A charge creates an electric field around it. The field points away from positive charges and toward negative charges.

Electric field:

\\[E=\\frac{k|Q|}{r^2}\\]
Force on a test charge:
\\[\\vec F=q\\vec E\\]
Gauss’s law:
\\[\\oint_S\\vec E\\cdot d\\vec A=\\frac{Q_{\\mathrm{enc}}}{\\varepsilon_0}\\]

Explore an electric field

Example: Doubling the distance from a point charge reduces the field to one-fourth. This is because distance is squared.

Gauss's law: Imagine an invisible closed balloon around a charge. The net electric flux through the balloon depends on the total charge inside, not the balloon's exact shape. A symmetric sphere makes the field easier to calculate.
