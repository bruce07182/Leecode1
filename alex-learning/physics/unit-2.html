Unit 2 — Electric Potential

Essentials · 30-second summary

  • Electric potential V is energy per unit charge; voltage is a potential difference.
  • A point charge creates V = kQ/r; potential is a scalar.
  • Electric field points downhill in potential: Eₓ = −dV/dx.

Key ideas

Only the essentials are shown. Tap a topic to learn more, reveal a hint, or check the answer.

Potential vs. field · §24-1–2

Remember: Electric field is force per charge; electric potential is energy per charge.

Why / when to use this

Field is a vector (direction matters); potential is a scalar. Voltage means Vfinal − Vinitial.

Practice question

Try: two points have potentials 12 V and 5 V. What is the change from 12 V to 5 V?

Show answer and explanation

ΔV = 5 − 12 = −7 V.

Point charges and superposition · §24-3

Remember: For one point charge, V = kQ/r.

Why / when to use this

For many point charges, add their signed potentials algebraically. Unlike electric field, you do not resolve directions.

Practice question

Try: +4 V and −6 V contributions at one point. Total potential?

Show answer and explanation

−2 V.

Dipoles and continuous charge · §24-4–5

Remember: A dipole has equal opposite charges; a distributed charge requires summing many tiny contributions.

Why / when to use this

For continuous distributions, integrate dV = k dq/r. This is often easier than integrating field vectors.

Practice question

Try: what is the potential at the midpoint of an equal +Q and −Q dipole?

Show answer and explanation

Zero: the two equal-magnitude contributions cancel.

Potential and electric field · §24-2, 24-6

Remember: A field points toward lower potential.

Why / when to use this

Along x, Eₓ = −dV/dx. Equipotential surfaces are perpendicular to the electric field.

Practice question

Try: V falls by 20 V over 4 m along +x in a uniform field. Find Eₓ.

Show answer and explanation

Eₓ = −(−20/4) = +5 V/m.

Potential energy and conductors · §24-7–8

Remember: For a charge q, U = qV and ΔU = qΔV.

Why / when to use this

In electrostatic equilibrium a conductor is at one potential and the field inside the conducting material is zero.

Practice question

Try: a +2 C charge moves through ΔV = −3 V. What is ΔU?

Show answer and explanation

ΔU = qΔV = −6 J.

What do the symbols mean?

Tap any symbol to see its definition and units.

VElectric potential

Electric potential energy per unit charge, measured in volts (V = J/C).

kCoulomb constant

Approximately 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².

QSource charge

The charge creating the potential, measured in coulombs (C).

rDistance

Distance from the point charge, in meters (m).

UElectric potential energy

Energy due to the arrangement of electric charges, measured in joules (J).

qTest charge

Charge placed at the potential, measured in coulombs (C).

ΔChange

Final value minus initial value.

EₓElectric field x-component

Electric field along the x direction, measured in N/C or V/m.

dV/dxPotential gradient

How quickly electric potential changes with position along x.

−Negative sign

The electric field points toward decreasing electric potential.

Move away from a positive charge

Example: A +2 nC source charge gives approximately 18 V at 1 m and 9 V at 2 m. Doubling distance halves potential, but quarters electric field.
